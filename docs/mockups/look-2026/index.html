Five new looks for Balance.

Each is a whole dashboard drawn with the same sample month — 4 100 € in, 2 074 € out, eating out at more than twice its usual — so you can hold them against each other and against the five directions in the folder above. Nothing here touches the real database.

Look 01
Quiet Ledger
White paper, hairline rules, no cards at all. Space and lines do the grouping, so nothing competes with the numbers. One red, spent only on the thing that broke its normal.
Biggest move: deletes the card chrome and opens with one sentence about the month.
Look 02
Baseline First
The medians you already compute become the layout. Every category is drawn as this month against its own normal, and the page opens with the app's verdict in a full sentence.
Biggest move: the comparison stops being a column and becomes the design.
Look 03
Warm Ink
A dark mode that reads instead of glowing: warm charcoal, cream text, sage and amber. High contrast, low saturation — nothing about it says trading terminal.
Biggest move: fixes the dark theme rather than the layout. Least disruptive of the five.
Look 04
Command Canvas
The sidebar becomes a 46-pixel bar and the window is all data. The filter reads as a sentence of chips you take apart one at a time, and ⌘K goes anywhere without the mouse.
Biggest move: reclaims 220px of width and makes the whole app keyboard-first.
Look 05
Glance
One number owns the screen and everything else is sized by how much it matters. The month is a single stacked bar; the category card lists the three that misbehaved, not all 34.
Biggest move: ranks the page by importance instead of giving every card equal weight.
Every one keeps the green accent, the fi-FI whole-euro formatting and the median-baseline idea — they differ in how much chrome the data has to fight, and in what the page says first. Pick one (or parts of several) and I will build it against the real CSS.
