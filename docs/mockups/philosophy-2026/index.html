Balance. — round 3

Six changes of mind, not of paint

The last two rounds asked what the app should look like. This one asks what it should be for. Balance today is an instrument panel: it lays out everything it knows, at equal weight, and leaves the reading to you. Every panel below is the same data — May: 2 340 € out, 4 100 € in — under a different idea of what the app owes its reader. Left is what ships today. Right is the proposal.

01

Lead with the verdict, not the ledger

Five summary cards at the same size say five things of equal importance, which means the page says nothing first. The app already computes enough to have an opinion — it knows the month, the average, the direction. Let it open with a sentence, then the number that sentence is about, then the rest at a smaller size.

02

Every number carries its own baseline

"Groceries 612 €" is not information — it's a fact waiting for a comparison. Is that a lot? The app is the only thing in the room that knows, and it doesn't say. Put normal on the same track as actual, as a tick mark, and the bar stops being a length and starts being a judgement.

03

One time control, in one place

The Dashboard has six separate ways to choose a span of time: the horizon buttons, the period picker, a scope toggle on each of the two breakdown cards, the trend category picker, and the heatmap's year dropdown. Each carries its own scoping rules — and CLAUDE.md already records the bug where two of them disagreed on screen. Six controls is not flexibility; it's six chances for the page to contradict itself.

04

The page should get shorter when nothing happened

Every card renders at full height whether or not it has anything to report. A month where nothing moved looks exactly as busy as a month where you overspent by 40 %. A card with no news should shrink to one line — and earn its full size back the moment it has something to say.

05

Colour is a judgement, not a category

Green and red currently mean income and expense — a fact you already know from the label. That spends the loudest signal the interface has on the least surprising thing in it, and leaves nothing to mark the one number that actually deserves attention. The Trends page already got this right ("a rising salary painted red reads as a warning about earning more"). The Dashboard hasn't caught up.

06

Three depths, not one long scroll

The Dashboard is six full-width cards in a column, each the same width and roughly the same weight, so reading it is a scroll through equals. But the three questions you actually bring to it are different sizes: am I fine? (two seconds), what changed? (a minute), why? (ten). Give each its own depth, and let the first one finish above the fold.

The through-line: Balance knows more than it says. Six of these changes are the app spending what it already computes — the median, the deviation, the price_changed flag, breakdownPeriodMonths() — on having a point of view instead of laying out evidence.

Cheapest to most: 05 (palette re-point) · 06 (re-order + collapse) · 03 (delete five controls) · 01 (hero block) · 02 (one median column on the breakdown endpoint) · 04 (a news rule per card).

Sample data throughout: May 2026 — 4 100 € in, 2 340 € out, 6-month median expense 2 610 €.
